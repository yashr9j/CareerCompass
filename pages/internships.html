Internships & Job Hunting Guide

Internships are the single most effective way to bridge the gap between academic knowledge and industry expectations. This page gives you a complete strategy — where to look, when to apply, how to stand out, and which companies are actively hiring students from tier-2 and tier-3 colleges.

Where to Find Internships

Top Platforms for Indian Students

  • Internshala — Largest student internship platform in India; apply with profile alone for many roles
  • LinkedIn — Essential for networking; set Job Alerts for "intern" roles in your city
  • Unstop (formerly D2C) — Competitions, hackathons, and internships bundled together
  • AngelList / Wellfound — Startup internships with equity potential and real responsibility
  • Naukri.com — Large job board; also has a fresher/intern section
  • HackerEarth / HackerRank Jobs — Applied via coding assessment; great for tech roles
  • Company Career Pages — Apply directly to Microsoft, Google, Amazon, Adobe, Flipkart, etc.

Research Internship Programs (Highly Competitive)

  • SRFP (SERB) — Science and Engineering Research Board, Govt of India summer fellowships
  • IIT Research Internships — Email professors directly with a CV and statement of purpose
  • IISc NIUS / Summer School — Top research exposure for motivated students
  • MITACS Globalink — Fully funded research internship in Canada (apply in Jan–Feb)
  • DAAD WISE — Research internship in Germany, fully funded (apply in Nov–Jan)

How to Stand Out as a Fresher

  1. Build before you apply — Have at least 2 deployed projects on GitHub with a proper README. Recruiters screen GitHub profiles before even reading resumes.
  2. Customize every application — Change your resume summary and cover letter for each company. Generic applications get ignored.
  3. Cold email professors and founders — A polite, specific email to a professor or startup founder asking for research or project work has a surprisingly high success rate.
  4. Win or participate in hackathons — Hackathon projects demonstrate initiative, speed, and teamwork. Many companies recruit directly from hackathons.
  5. Contribute to open source — Even one merged pull request on a popular GitHub repository is a significant differentiator on a fresher resume.
  6. Get LinkedIn active — Post about what you are learning and building. Recruiters do find students who share content consistently.

Typical Internship Interview Process

  1. Online Assessment (OA) — 2–3 DSA coding problems on HackerRank / HackerEarth (60–90 min)
  2. Technical Round 1 — DSA problem solving on a shared editor, concept questions
  3. Technical Round 2 — System design basics (for experienced interns) or project deep-dive
  4. HR / Culture Fit Round — Behavioral questions, motivation, availability
  5. Offer Letter — Usually sent within 1–2 weeks of final round

Note: Startups often skip formal rounds — a portfolio review and 30-minute conversation may be all it takes. Mass recruiters (TCS, Infosys) rely heavily on aptitude scores. Prepare differently for each company type.
